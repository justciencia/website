Ever wonder why a lemon makes your face scrunch up while soap feels slippery? Both come down to one number: the **pH**.

## A number from 0 to 14

pH tells us how acidic or basic something is. Picture it as a scale with three zones:

- **Below 7:** acidic. Lemon juice, vinegar, coffee.
- **Exactly 7:** neutral. Pure water is the classic example.
- **Above 7:** basic, also called alkaline. Soap, baking soda, bleach.

The farther a number is from 7, the stronger it is. And watch out: each step is **10 times** stronger than the one before. pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4, and 100 times more acidic than pH 5.

## How do we measure it?

One easy way is an indicator: something that changes color depending on the pH, like the colors in the beaker above. Another way is a pH meter, which uses a glass electrode to sense a tiny voltage and turns it into a number. In other words, a pH meter is really a little voltage sensor.
